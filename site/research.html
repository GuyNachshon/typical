Typical

Research

The decision lives in the model before it emits a letter; we read it there instead of generating. The negative result matters as much: candidate-blind states carry priors and calibration, not question-conditioned knowledge (§3j, §3s). Architecture, data, pipeline, and the full result set below — including the numbers that did not move.

Section references (§…) point to the project's internal REPORT.md, which is not yet public; the Hugging Face model cards reproduce the tables cited here.

01 · Architecture

One forward pass, three typed heads

A frozen Qwen3 trunk cut at roughly 71% depth, LoRA on the top 8 kept layers, and a small contextual readout with a factored ∅ head. The state is read once and cached; each query appends a short rendered suffix to that cache. Nothing is generated — the label, the yes/no, and the level all come out of the same terminal decision state.

a) Why mid-depth, not the last layer

The first version of this project (a candidate-blind decision vector, frozen 0.6B backbone) read the trunk's last layer and quietly built a hypothesis-only model: SNLI accuracy landed at 58.6%, exactly the score a model gets from the hypothesis alone, ignoring the premise (REPORT §2.4). Moving the tap to layer 20 of 28 and adding LoRA fixed it (65.3% frozen, 68.2% with LoRA) — the premise finally mattered. The same question came back for the native, candidate-aware readout: is full depth (28 of 28) better once the candidates are inside the computation too? It is not. Matched runs at layer 28 vs. layer 20 (same N3 readout, same data, same steps) show tap 20 ahead on every evidence task — SNLI .906 vs. .863, MNLI .865 vs. .752, ANLI .519 vs. .431, BoolQ .834 vs. .737 (REPORT §3r) — while keeping the question-dependent signal (Δ_q .118 vs. .117 at 28 layers). Depth is not free capacity here: for a candidate-aware readout, the last third of the trunk is where evidence reasoning gets lost, not where it accumulates.

b) Why there is no rendered "none of the above" line

An earlier version rendered ∅ as a literal candidate in the suffix, the way a multiple-choice prompt might add "E. none of the above." That line was itself the failure mode: null probability collapsed to always-abstain at large label sets (K=150 CLINC, K=77 Banking77) and never-abstain at small ones (K=5), and MMLU-Pro false-abstain sat at .692. Making ∅ a separate head decision instead — letters_nonull plus a factored null — fixed the K-sweep (smooth and monotone at every K), dropped MMLU-Pro false-abstain to .003, and took Banking77-77 accuracy from .063 (unusable) to .579, with Δ_q unchanged at .122 and no cost to NLI/BoolQ (REPORT §3t). The cost is real but smaller: topic/intent label spaces lose 2–7 points (HWU64 .801 → .757, 20NG .589 → .515) and choice-set null probes lose some AUROC — a genuine trade, not a free win.

c) Why the candidates have to be inside the computation

A separate line of experiments tried to compile a listwise teacher's knowledge into a candidate-blind decision state Z(x, q) — computed before the candidate set is known, so one state could in principle serve any question. A choices-only control (state = ".", labels only) showed why that fails: every student architecture tested — a single vector, 8 probes, 8 probes with set-conditioning, under both single-set and same-Z multi-set distillation with an explicit Δ-log-odds objective — scored the same or better with the real question replaced by a shuffled one. None of their above-chance accuracy was question-dependent; all of it was candidate-set priors and calibration, learned uniformly across every head shape (REPORT §3j). Re-running the same student at full depth (28 layers, not just tap 20) closed the question: Δ_q stayed at −0.008, within noise of zero, while evidence tasks regressed exactly as the depth result above predicts (REPORT §3s). What compiles into a candidate-blind state is priors and calibration; question-conditioned knowledge does not, at any tap. That is why Typical's candidates — Choice's label set, Score's levels — are rendered into the suffix and read by the same forward pass as the question, not looked up afterward against a fixed state.

02 · Data

Four buckets
sampled per batch

E .40 / K .15 / W .35 / U .10. The workflow corpora are ours and programmatic; held-out families, grammars, styles, and an untrained level-7 tier measure transfer, not memorisation.

Family weights and corpus row counts (releases/typical-small.md, Training args + Data section). kb 147,446 rows (MMLU-aux/AQuA/MedMCQA/ LogiQA-style distillation) · wf 72,000 rows / 12 families, our rubric-conditioned workflow corpus · wf_hf 86,600 rows from three HF-sourced workflow sets · wh 60,605 rows, DecisionMix v2 · u 31,029 rows, soft-target uncertainty corpus.

DecisionMix v2 (bucket W, data_wh)

A programmatic rule engine (scripts/decisionmix_v2.py): 12 domains × 6 boolean conditions, rendered at levels 1–7, with 100% of training rows drawn in counterfactual rubric groups (same state and candidates, at least two distinct golds across the group — the same facts, a different rule, a different answer). Held out entirely from training: one domain/level pair (wh_heldout_grammar), three whole domains (wh_heldout_family), two rubric styles (wh_heldout_style), and level 7 — temporal, numeric, probability, and trade-off composition — which is eval-only and never trained on. A leak audit against JevBench's 231 public items found 0/231 hits.

The uncertainty corpus (bucket U, data_u)

31,029 soft-target training rows, same generator family. Real sources: UNLI's validation split (train/test are consumed elsewhere in the mix) and the ambiguous rows of metaeval/ambient (uniform target over the listed labels); metaeval/chaos-mnli-ambiguity is eval-only, never trained on. The remaining ~92% comes from four synthetic generators with exact closed-form targets — partial evidence, a noisy-sensor Bayes posterior, ordinal confusion, and conflicting-sources log-odds — each with a held-out parameter range. Also 0/231 JevBench leak hits.

Null augmentation

--null_aug W:0.20 — 15–20% of workflow rows have their gold candidate removed at train time so the target becomes ∅, teaching abstention on rows that would otherwise always have an answer. Combined with the factored-∅ head design (§01b above), this is what keeps the null calibrated across K instead of collapsing at the extremes.

03 · Pipeline

The training recipe

88 training, baseline, and ablation run directories sit under runs/ on the source branch across six days (2026-09-16 → 09-21), most of them well under $20 of H100 time. The line moves because of bugs found and controls run, not because of scale — see the decision log below.

typical-medium (4B) uses the same recipe on Qwen3-4B-Base, tap 26/36, effective batch 64 via --grad_accum 4 (releases/typical-medium.md). Final val NLL 0.376, temperature T = 1.124, null_offset 0.0 (typical-small; fit post-hoc on the val split, applied at eval).

Cost, where REPORT states it

typical-small (ts1b): ~$12 of H100 time, plus ~$10 lost to a routing bug caught and fixed mid-run (REPORT §3ae). typical-medium (tm1b): ~$15, plus ~$8 lost to the same bug class (REPORT §3af). The DecisionMix v2 dry run that preceded them (r1_dmv2) was ~$12 (REPORT §3ad). Wall-clock and per-run cost for the 4B/8B/14B scaling ladder are not separately itemized in REPORT §3ab, so they are left out here rather than estimated.

Eval suite

Evidence: SNLI, MNLI, ANLI, BoolQ. Topic/intent: CLINC-150, HWU64, 20 Newsgroups, TREC-fine. Knowledge: MMLU-Pro among-K, Δ_q (shuffled-question control), TruthfulQA-MC1. Typed/workflow, held-out: noul, score, style, and DecisionMix v2's family/grammar/style/level-7/rubric-flip. Uncertainty: ChaosNLI, real and synthetic held-out sets from the U corpus. External, never trained on: PagerDuty, jevlogs, Mind2Web, tree-choice (K=320), typed-decisions. Public benchmark: JevBench (231-item public subset; see the D1 disclosure in §05 below).

Eight established results

Each with a matched control, and where it mattered, a seed pair or a full-row re-evaluation (REPORT §6).

01 Candidate-blind states carry priors and calibration, not question-dependent knowledge. Holds at tap 20 and at full depth. A candidate-aware suffix computation keeps the knowledge; a direct contextual readout keeps it with a quarter of the letter interface's order fragility. REPORT §3j, §3l, §3s, §3v
02 The evidence-vs-knowledge trade-off was two artifacts: last-layer tap and a rendered "none of the above" line. Removing both gives one model (nc_v3_tap20, research checkpoint, not released): evidence at energy level, Δ_q kept, null monotone in K, JevBench standard .694 with no workflow training. REPORT §3r, §3t
03 Rubric-conditioned workflow data teaches the decision shapes it contains, not general external abstraction. Held-out rubric styles +34, held-out families +9–14, rubric-flip both-correct .43 → .57 — while jevlogs / PagerDuty / Mind2Web stayed at their constant-prediction floor for K-way Choice until the typed heads and DecisionMix v2 arrived (findings 06–07 below). REPORT §3w, §3x
04 Mixing is a first-class variable, not a detail. E .45–.50 restores evidence; ∅-augmented W rows fix abstention and MMLU where a single eval-time null threshold cannot serve both E and W at once; the exact W fraction in [.1, .2] does not matter once null augmentation is on. REPORT §3y, §3z, §3aa
05 Small batch = under-fitting = better hard-tier and soft-target numbers, at the cost of everything in-distribution. At 1.7B, the JevBench hard tier is a probability-quality problem, not a data-volume one. REPORT §3aa
06 Typed primitives: ordinal-smoothed Score and a Bernoulli Noul head both win on probability quality, at zero or negative decision-accuracy cost. Ordinal smoothing (τ=0.7) changes no JevBench decision while held-out score NLL goes 2.07 → 1.23 (intermediate checkpoints, §3ac; released models' held-out score NLL: 1.01 / 1.02). The Bernoulli Noul head is exactly order-invariant by construction and is the first thing to clear an untouched external floor by a margin: PagerDuty .602 → .886 (intermediate checkpoint, §3ac; released models .817 / .838 vs floor .792). REPORT §3ac
07 DecisionMix v2's hard curriculum transfers within its own rule grammar, not beyond it. Held-out families/grammars +34–37, rubric-flip .48 → .71, JevBench adversarial .33 → .83 (intermediate checkpoints r1_cand → r1_dmv2, §3ad; not released — the released models' equivalent wh_heldout numbers are in the held-out chart below: typical-small .836/.898/.896, typical-medium .874/.893/.891) — while level-7 composition (temporal / unit / expected-value / trade-off) sits at ~.50, chance, for every checkpoint on the ladder. The U corpus buys likelihood, not top-1 accuracy. REPORT §3ad
08 Same recipe at scale: 1.7B → 4B → 14B lifts standard-tier accuracy, knowledge, and in-distribution workflow decisions monotonically, at roughly constant latency. JevBench standard .750 → .833 → .875, MMLU-Pro among-K .330 → .457 → .514, held-out noul .70 → .84 → .89, at 45 → 56 → 60 ms per decision (scaling-ladder checkpoints, §3ab, same pre-DecisionMix-v2 recipe at every size — none released; typical-small/typical-medium use a later recipe and score .694 / .806 on JevBench standard). 8B is a checkpoint outlier in both frozen and trained form, not part of the trend. Long-state families and soft-target calibration do not scale — the frozen 14B with three shots beats the trained 14B on the hard tier (.559 vs. .468) — so the hard tier is a data/objective problem at every size, not a capacity one. REPORT §3ab

Held-out curriculum vs. external, never-trained sets

The sets below split into two kinds: one is inside DecisionMix v2's own grammar (held out by family, style, or grammar, plus level 7 kept in on purpose as an untrained-composition control — it sits at chance for every checkpoint). The other is never trained on at all: real logging/ticketing/web-agent corpora, scored against a constant-prediction floor.

JevBench, per family (typical-small)

D1 — JevBench disclosure (REPORT §3q). JevBench numbers are a public-subset run against fstandhartinger/jevbench v1.2.1 (72 standard / 48 easy / 111 hard public ids), not a submitted or ranked leaderboard entry (the leaderboard requires ≥95% coverage including 146 non-public judge items). Knowledge retained from the backbone

MMLU-Pro among-K, Δ_q (shuffled-question control), and TruthfulQA-MC1 measure how much of the frozen backbone's knowledge survives the fine-tune — they are not a selling point. typical-small: among-K .343, Δ_q .127, TruthfulQA .257. typical-medium: among-K .458, Δ_q .193, TruthfulQA .408 (REPORT §3af table). Compare to level-7 composition (§03 above), which the training data does not touch at all and which stays at chance regardless of size.

06 · Limitations

What we do not claim

Re-derived for typical-small and typical-medium specifically, not inherited from any prior write-up.

  • We do not claim to be faster, better, or cheaper than Jev or any hosted system — there is no apples-to-apples run, and JevBench is unranked for us.
  • We do not do vision, audio, real-time control, game-playing skill, or planning/lookahead. Text-only, one forward pass, no search.
  • We are not a knowledge model and do not reason: MMLU-Pro sits at .34 / .46 among-K, and level-7 composition (temporal, units, expected value, trade-off) is at chance (.495 / .544, floor .46). We do not do arithmetic, timezones, or trade-offs.
  • We do not handle long documents. Trained at ≤1,024-token states; JevBench long_policy is .26 / .21; the preview checkpoint's hard tier goes from .64 at ≤256 tokens to .23 above 1,024.
  • We do not claim "any label set, any domain." CLINC is −4 versus the untrained base; TREC-fine is .508 / .414 (typical-medium is −10 versus its own untyped control); held-out rubric styles regress −4 / −6.
  • We do not claim exact order-invariance for Choice, or exact independence of irrelevant alternatives. Choice is order-robust (mean-max Δp .01–.08), not order-invariant — only Noul is exact. Adding an irrelevant candidate moves Δlog-odds .09–.22.
  • We do not claim out-of-distribution calibration or hard-tier competence. Both released models are at chance on the JevBench hard tier, with Brier .79 / .77.
  • We do not ship 3 open sizes. There are two backbone sizes (1.7B, 4B) and three checkpoints (typical-small-preview is also 1.7B). There is no typical-large — the 14B point is a scaling-ladder measurement, not a release.
  • Training code is not released. Only inference/ is public today; training code release to follow.
  • We do not claim JevBench standard improved from typical-small to the preview — it went .750 → .694 (~1 SE), traded for calibration and the typed heads.
  • We do not natively serve very large candidate sets (hundreds to thousands) at this head — that needs an energy → top-r → native path, not shipped yet. The native suffix budget is 1,024 tokens by default.
  • We do not claim to beat supervised classifiers on CLINC, Banking77, or TREC.
  • jevlogs numbers are not for commercial use (research-licensed, block-level labels, held-out sanity eval only). The licenses of metaeval/ambient and metaeval/chaos-mnli-ambiguity are undeclared on their HF cards — flagged here, not asserted.

07 · Reproduce

pip install -r inference/requirements.txt
from typical import Typical  # inference/typical/, downloaded alongside your code

m = Typical.from_pretrained("OzLabs/typical-small", device="auto")

# K-way choice over a fixed label set -> {label: p, ...} + p_null
m.choice(state, "What does the customer want?", ["refund", "replacement", "repair"])

# Yes/no -> P(yes)
m.noul(state, "Is the order still under warranty?")

# Ordinal levels -> {level: p, ...} + p_null + expected (E[index])
m.score(state, "How urgent is this ticket?", ["0", "1", "2", "3"])
