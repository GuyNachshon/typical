\section{Speed, scale and probability quality}
\label{sec:scale}

The architecture fixed in \Cref{sec:depends,sec:invariant} is the same at every backbone size. This
section measures it against frozen backbones, in latency and in probability quality.

\subsection{Speed}
\label{sec:results:cost}

Decision latency is nearly flat in backbone size. On the research harness a single decision at
$\numcands{=}2$ costs 45, 56 and 60\,ms at 1.7B, 4B and 14B: an $8\times$ larger backbone is
$1.3\times$ slower, because the cached state and a short suffix dominate (\Cref{app:latency-sweep}).
Cost stays flat to $\numcands{=}32$ and rises at $\numcands{=}256$, where the rendered candidates
dominate the suffix (\Cref{sec:method:cost}).

On the serving path a warm decision takes 15.5--17\,ms at 1.7B and 19--21\,ms for the Qwen3-4B model
(warm p50, $\numcands{=}2$, \Cref{fig:serving}; Qwen3.5-4B in \Cref{app:serving}). Reading the candidates inside the forward pass
(\Cref{sec:analysis:deltaq}) has a price in throughput mode: with many questions against one cached
state, a candidate-blind energy scorer costs about 1\,ms per question at every $\numcands$ and is
more than $3\times$ cheaper than the contextual readout from $\numcands{=}32$ on, measured on an earlier
full-depth prototype (\Cref{app:latency-sweep}).

\subsection{Probability quality}
\label{sec:results:calibration}

Probability quality is indexed by decision type, so the training data, the target and checkpoint
selection set it. The ordinal Score target shows the effect of the target alone. In the matched
fine-tunes of \Cref{sec:results:typed}, the ordinal-smoothed target ($\tauord = 0.7$) adds no parameters
and improves every probability-quality measure on held-out urgency at flat accuracy (.505 $\to$ .508):
NLL 2.07 $\to$ 1.23, Brier .79 $\to$ .68, ECE .35 $\to$ .19, ordinal MAE .58 $\to$ .55. On JevBench's 12
ordinal items the two models make identical per-item predictions, so the gain is in how mass is spread
over adjacent levels.

A single post-hoc correction cannot do the same job: a global temperature and null-logit offset fit on
held-out soft-target rows want opposite corrections for an evidence-and-knowledge and a workflow-trained
checkpoint (\Cref{app:calibration-fit}), so we make the correction in data and objective instead, through null augmentation and per-type targets.

On the 14B backbone the recipe bundle (facts-first long states with truncated rows dropped, a
3{,}072-token window, DecisionMix v2 and the uncertainty corpus, typed heads with the ordinal target,
checkpoint selection on held-out uncertainty and curriculum NLL, and frozen-teacher distillation) takes
held-out Score NLL from 2.87 under the earlier workflow recipe to 0.95, typed-decisions NLL from 1.96 to
1.04, and JevBench hard-tier Brier from .85 to .66 (\Cref{fig:calibration}). No component is isolated
except distillation, and the arm without it reaches the same Score NLL. The v1 1.7B and 4B checkpoints,
which have the ordinal target and the uncertainty corpus but no likelihood-based selection, already
reach 1.01 and 1.03; all 1.7B and 4B checkpoints with typed heads and DecisionMix sit at 0.97--1.08.
