\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Limitations.} We list these explicitly rather than folding them into the results.
\textit{Benchmark scope.} Every JevBench number in this paper is measured on the benchmark's public
231-item subset and is not a ranked leaderboard entry; the 146 judge-scored items required for ranking are
not public, and the effective sample size at the standard tier is $n_{\mathrm{eff}}{=}36$
(\Cref{sec:experiments:jevbench}). \textit{Single seeds.} Outside the explicit two-seed comparisons of
\Cref{sec:results:readout}, every number in this paper is a single training run; noise-floor measurements
elsewhere in this project put NLI/BoolQ variation at $\pm$0.7 points and held-out label-space or
out-of-scope variation at $\pm$3--5 points between identically configured seeds, which should be treated as
the resolution below which we do not claim a difference. \textit{One backbone family per ladder point.}
The 1.7B/4B/8B/14B scaling ladder is entirely within the Qwen3 family; the single Qwen3.5-4B checkpoint is
suggestive that backbone family matters independently of size (\Cref{sec:analysis:tension}) but is one
data point, not a second ladder -- a Qwen3.5-9B checkpoint (\texttt{tl2}) was trained but not yet evaluated
at the time of writing, and we omit it rather than report it provisionally. \textit{No judge items.} We
never evaluate against JevBench's private, judge-scored items, so we cannot rule out that our public-subset
numbers differ systematically from a full-benchmark ranking. \textit{Evaluation-only external sets have
soft or constructed gold.} PagerDuty, jevlogs, Mind2Web, tree-choice, and typed-decisions are real-world
or realistic decision sets, but several rely on rule-based or LLM-assisted gold labels rather than
human adjudication (\Cref{sec:experiments:external}); we report accuracy against each set's own
majority-class floor specifically so that a below-floor result is legible as a genuine failure rather than
an artifact of class imbalance, but the gold itself was not independently re-verified by us.
\textit{The 8B anomaly.} Qwen3-8B is an outlier in both its frozen and trained form under the identical
recipe used at every other ladder point (\Cref{sec:results:headline}); we did not find a tap or
learning-rate configuration that recovers it within budget, and it should be read as a checkpoint-specific
anomaly rather than evidence about 8B-parameter backbones generally. \textit{Runs in flight.} Three
checkpoints (\texttt{ts1b\_semif}, \texttt{tl1b\_semif}, and the Qwen3.5-9B \texttt{tl2}) were training or
awaiting evaluation at the time of writing and are omitted from every table rather than reported
provisionally. \textit{Latency methodology.} Every latency number (\Cref{sec:results:latency}) comes from
a single-stream, Python-level harness with no comparison against an optimized serving runtime or a
saturated multi-request server; we report these as engineering evidence that the architecture is viable at
these sizes, not as tuned lower bounds. \textit{Bundled fixes.} The 14B long-state result
(\Cref{sec:results:kd}) combines five changes in one run with no matched ablation isolating the
facts-first truncation fix specifically, and the frozen-vs-trained hard-tier comparison
(\Cref{sec:analysis:tension}) is not controlled for state/option rendering, which we separately show can
move a frozen model's score by a comparable margin.

\textbf{Conclusion.} We showed that a pretrained causal language model can be read as a typed, calibrated
decision engine without generating any output tokens: a state is encoded once and cached, a query and a
runtime-defined candidate set are rendered as a short causal suffix against that cache, and a direct
contextual readout over the terminal decision state and each candidate's own contextual representation
recovers essentially all of the question-conditioned knowledge of a letter-generating interface while
removing most of its order sensitivity, at 45--60\,ms per decision independent of backbone size. Reaching
this required diagnosing and fixing three specific failure modes -- a last-layer tap that discards the
state entirely, a rendered abstain option that dilutes under softmax competition as the candidate set
grows, and a truncation bug that silently drops the evidence for exactly the long-context decisions it was
meant to teach -- none of which is visible from aggregate accuracy alone. Once fixed, the same recipe
scales close to monotonically with backbone size on standard-tier accuracy, knowledge, and in-distribution
workflow decisions (JevBench standard .694 at 1.7B, .931 at 14B; held-out score calibration NLL 2.87 to
0.95 after calibration-shaped checkpoint selection and frozen-teacher distillation), while a harder tier of
compositional, multi-step decisions remains at or near chance for every model we trained, including the
frozen backbone with in-context exemplars, and remains the clearest open target for future work.
