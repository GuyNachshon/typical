\section{What the decision should not depend on}
\label{sec:invariant}
\label{sec:results}

A decision should stay put when the order of the labels, the number of options offered or the layout of
the input changes. Each subsection gives the failure a natural design shows, the perturbation that
exposed it and the change that removed or bounded it.

\subsection{Label order}
\label{sec:results:typed}

The decision should not change when the same labels appear in a different order. A yes/no decision
rendered as a two-way Choice fails this test. ``Yes'' and ``no'' occupy two different suffix positions
with two different contextual states, so the rendering carries order into the scores, and reversing the
labels of a two-way Choice over \{no, yes\} moves $P(\text{yes})$ by up to .55, and by .01--.15 on
average across sets (\Cref{app:typed-primitives}). Order invariance alone is easy to obtain: the candidate-blind variants of
\Cref{sec:analysis:deltaq} are exactly set-stable and carry no question dependence
(\Cref{app:extra-figures}).

Each output type delivers the property its contract names. Matched fine-tunes compare each type
against a control that treats the same rows as plain Choice (\Cref{app:typed-primitives}). The Noul
Bernoulli head renders no candidates (\Cref{sec:method:typed}), so label order cannot enter its
computation. For rows routed to it, reversing the labels
moves $P(\text{yes})$ by exactly zero on all nine test sets. The head is better or equal on six of seven
yes/no sets and clears the untouched PagerDuty floor by a wide margin: accuracy .602 $\to$ .886 against a
.792 majority floor, NLL 1.04 $\to$ .28. The contract holds only under per-row routing (\Cref{app:typed-primitives}).

Choice has no output index for any label and must read the candidates to score them
(\Cref{sec:method:task}), so the readout controls how much the order and the rest of the set move the
scores (\Cref{tab:n1n3}). N3 is far less sensitive to the
candidate set than the letter-logit readout. Adding three unrelated options shifts N3's pairwise
log-odds by .09--.13 against .44--.46 for N1, below the teacher's .179, and reordering moves its
probabilities by .10--.11 against .165. The difference comes from the readout together with its null
form; exact order invariance is the Bernoulli head's contract.

\subsection{How many options are offered}
\label{sec:analysis:null}

Whether the model abstains should depend on whether any offered candidate fits, and not on how many
candidates are offered. Abstention belongs in the gate alone. A rendered ``none of the above'' line
competes for softmax mass with the real candidates, so its share depends on how many there are. The
$\numcands$-sweep with the gold label absent exposes this. With the line, abstention has degenerate
endpoints: the model almost never abstains on CLINC-150 at $\numcands{=}5$ ($P(\nullsym \mid
\text{absent}) = .02$) and always abstains at $\numcands{=}150$ (1.00) and on Banking77 at
$\numcands{=}77$.

A matched pair tests the fix: both arms are N3 at layer 20 with the factored gate of
\Cref{eq:factored-null}, and the treatment removes the rendered $\nullsym$ line. Removing the line
removes both endpoints (null AUROC by $\numcands$ in \Cref{app:extra-figures}). MMLU-Pro false
abstention falls from .692 to .003 (far outside its seed-to-seed variation of .05--.10) and accuracy
rises from .133 to .353, Banking77 with all 77 labels rises from .063 to .579, and CLINC out-of-scope
accuracy from .639 to .798, with $\dqsh$ unchanged (.118 vs.\ .122) and evidence tasks level
(\Cref{tab:app-null-fix}). The price is 2--7 points on intent and
topic label spaces (HWU64 .801 $\to$ .757, 20NG .589 $\to$ .515) and a rise in reorder $\Delta p$ from
.077 to .123. This configuration, contextual readout at 71\% depth with a gate and no rendered
$\nullsym$ line, is the architecture of every checkpoint in this section and \Cref{sec:scale}.
The gate still has to learn when to fire, and the training distribution sets that. Decision-type
balance and null targets each move a separate axis from the rubric groups of \Cref{sec:results:mixture}.
Duplicating 20\% of workflow rows with the gold candidate removed and target $\nullsym$ lowers false
abstention on CLINC-150 from .10 to .08 and on TREC-fine from .27 to .12, and raises accuracy by 2.6,
3.6, 4.3 and 6.0 points on CLINC-150, TREC-fine, HWU64 and 20NG (\Cref{tab:app-mixture}). The model now spends mass
on $\nullsym$ where soft gold has none, so held-out Noul drops 4 points and held-out Score NLL rises
(1.52 $\to$ 2.04).

Family balance acts on false abstention as well. Adding workflow data at family weights E .35 /
K .25 / W .40 cost 11 points on CLINC-150 and 10 on TREC-fine, almost all of it false abstention
(CLINC-150 .05 $\to$ .17). In the mixture sweep, which ran without the uncertainty corpus or null
augmentation, $E \ge .45$ recovers CLINC-150 to .779 with false abstention .10 (\Cref{tab:app-mixture}). The release sampler, E .40 with
U .10, keeps every evidence set within about 1 point of the evidence-only base (\Cref{app:mixture}).

\subsection{Formatting}
\label{sec:results:rendering}

The decision should not change when the same content is laid out differently. Frozen models fail this
test at 4B and 9B. With identical
JevBench items, frozen Qwen3.5 checkpoints and letter-logit readout, a replication of another published
system's state and option layout lifts the standard tier by 1.4, 8.3 and 12.5 points at 2B, 4B and 9B,
surviving a paired per-item test at 4B ($p = .048$) and 9B ($p < .001$); no hard-tier change separates
(\Cref{tab:rendering}); the effect exceeds most of the spread between public JevBench entries
(\Cref{app:leaderboard}).

A trained model depends on the render it was trained in. At 1.7B,
render mismatch inside one trained model costs more than the training defect. In the off-diagonal cells
of \Cref{tab:twobytwo}, switching the evaluation render costs 14.5 points for the facts-first model and
19.0 for the facts-last one, which lands on its \texttt{policy\_permit} floor (.615 against .612). At
14B it costs 7.6 and 6.8 points, so the larger backbone tolerates render mismatch better.

Typical fixes the layout of the question and options in the suffix (\Cref{sec:method:pass}), but the
caller writes the state, and the render crossing of \Cref{sec:results:long}, which separates data
effects from compatibility effects, measures what a change of state layout costs. The frozen controls of \Cref{tab:headline} use a
letter-logit rendering we did not sweep, so frozen-versus-trained comparisons carry a rendering factor of
the size shown here.
