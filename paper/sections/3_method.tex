\section{Typical: the decision pass}
\label{sec:method}

Typical turns a pretrained causal language model into a decision function. A call supplies a state, a
typed question and a candidate set, and receives a probability for every candidate plus a probability of
abstention, read from hidden states in one pass over a short suffix. This section defines the task
(\Cref{sec:method:task}), the pass (\Cref{sec:method:pass}), the readout (\Cref{sec:method:readout}) and
the layer it reads (\Cref{sec:method:depth}), the typed heads (\Cref{sec:method:typed}), the abstention
gate (\Cref{sec:method:null}), and the cost model (\Cref{sec:method:cost}).

\subsection{Typed decisions}
\label{sec:method:task}

\begin{definition}[Typed decision]
\label{def:task}
A decision is a tuple $\decision = (\state, \query, \cands, \dtype)$: a state $\state$ (the document,
transcript or trace the decision is about), a question $\query$ (the instruction or rubric), a candidate
set $\cands = \{\cand{1},\dots,\cand{\numcands}\}$ of label strings supplied at request time, and a type
$\dtype \in \{\textsc{Choice}, \textsc{Score}, \textsc{Noul}\}$. The model returns
\[
  P(\cand{j} \mid \state, \query, \cands),\ j = 1,\dots,\numcands,
  \qquad
  \pnull = P(\nullsym \mid \state, \query, \cands),
  \qquad
  \pnull + \textstyle\sum_{j} P(\cand{j} \mid \state, \query, \cands) = 1,
\]
and emits no token.
\end{definition}

The type is a contract on the returned distribution, and each contract is enforced by the head and the
training target. \textbf{Choice} is categorical over $\cands$. \textbf{Score} is categorical over an
ordered $\cands$ (severity levels, priorities), trained so that mass on a level adjacent to the gold level
costs less than mass on a distant one. \textbf{Noul} is a Bernoulli probability for a single proposition
with $\cands = \{\text{yes}, \text{no}\}$, invariant to the order in which the caller lists the two
labels. The abstention event $\nullsym$ is available under every type and is never one of the
candidates. Because $\cands$ is text supplied per call, the model has no output index for any label and
must read the candidates to score them.

\subsection{Cached state and causal suffix}
\label{sec:method:pass}

The state is encoded once as an ordinary causal prefix and its key-value cache is retained. Each question
against it is a short causal suffix: the question text, the candidates rendered as lettered lines, an
answer cue, and a terminal decision token. The suffix runs through the backbone attending to the cached
state, and a batch of suffixes against one state runs in chunks without attending to one another. A
decision is therefore exactly a forward pass on $\mathrm{concat}(\state, \query, \cands)$ with the state
half read from cache. We verify this with an equivalence test on the real model: cached-state decisions
match the full-concatenation path to within $10^{-3}$ across suffix chunk sizes 1, 2, 3, 5 and 64, are
consistent under permutation of the queries in a batch, and repeat exactly. No question recomputes the
state, and no question sees another.

\subsection{The contextual-candidate readout}
\label{sec:method:readout}

One forward pass over the suffix yields two things at the tap layer: the terminal decision state $\hD$,
the hidden state at the decision token, and for each candidate a contextual state $\hctx{j}$, the mean of
the hidden states over the tokens of candidate $j$'s rendered span. Candidate $j$ receives the score
\begin{equation}
  u = W_h \hD, \qquad v_j = W_c\, \hctx{j}, \qquad
  s_j = \frac{u^{\top} v_j}{\sqrt{d_v}} + w^{\top}(u \odot v_j) + g(u \odot v_j),
  \label{eq:readout}
\end{equation}
where $W_h$ and $W_c$ are layer-normalized linear projections to width $d_v$, $w$ is a vector and $g$ a
two-layer MLP. The score is a bilinear form in $\hD$ and $\hctx{j}$ with a small learned correction on
their elementwise product. The option letters stay in the rendered text and are never read out.

Because the candidates are tokens in the same causal computation as the state and the question,
$\hctx{j}$ carries the candidate's meaning together with its slot and the set it appears in, and $\hD$
has attended to all of them. We compare three readouts that differ only in what $\hD$ is scored against
(\Cref{sec:analysis:readout}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{N1, letter-logit readout.} $\hD$ is scored against the output embedding of each option
  letter, i.e.\ the next-token logits of the letters. Nothing is generated. This is the standard
  multiple-choice interface read at the decision token.
  \item \textbf{N2, candidate-blind semantic readout.} $\hD$ is scored with \Cref{eq:readout} against an
  embedding of the candidate text computed independently by a frozen text-embedding model, which carries
  the label's meaning and no slot identity.
  \item \textbf{N3, contextual-candidate readout.} $\hD$ is scored with \Cref{eq:readout} against the
  span-pooled contextual state $\hctx{j}$. This is the readout Typical deploys.
\end{itemize}
N3 keeps N1's question-conditioned signal within seed noise, cuts its pairwise log-odds shift under
candidate-set edits to about a quarter, and gains on evidence tasks. N2, with the same scorer, keeps
$\dqsh$ of .058 against the teacher's .102 and scores .012 on Banking77 at $\numcands = 77$
(\Cref{sec:analysis:readout}).

\subsection{Mid-depth tap}
\label{sec:method:depth}

The backbone is truncated at about 71\% of its depth: the readout taps layer 20 of 28 at 1.7B, 26 of 36 at
4B and 8B, and 28 of 40 at 14B. The layers above the tap are removed from the model, so the top 8 layers at
1.7B, 10 at 4B and 8B, and 12 at 14B are never computed for any token, state or suffix. LoRA \citep{hu2022lora}
at rank 16 adapts the top eight kept layers (layers 13--20 at 1.7B), and everything below is frozen.

The tap is chosen for what the representation holds. Our first full-scale model read the final layer
and scored 58.6 on SNLI, the hypothesis-only baseline: it ignored the premise. For the contextual
readout, moving the tap from layer 28 to layer 20 with everything else fixed raises SNLI, MNLI, ANLI and
BoolQ from .863, .752, .431 and .737 to .906, .865, .519 and .834, and keeps the question-conditioned
signal ($\dqsh$ .118 against .117), so one model carries both evidence and knowledge
(\Cref{sec:analysis:depth}). All released and ladder models use this tap.

\subsection{Typed heads}
\label{sec:method:typed}

Choice, Score and Noul share the backbone, the tap and the readout of \Cref{eq:readout}. They differ in
the training target and, for Noul, the terminal head, and every row is routed by its own type.

\textbf{Choice} is the $\numcands$-way readout trained with cross-entropy against the gold distribution
over $\cands \cup \{\nullsym\}$, composed through the gate of \Cref{sec:method:null}.

\textbf{Score} uses the Choice readout over the rendered levels with an ordinal-smoothed target. For gold
level $y$,
\[
  \tilde{t}_j \propto \exp\!\left(-\frac{|j - y|}{\tauord}\right), \qquad \tauord = 0.7,
\]
normalized over the valid levels; $\tauord \to 0$ recovers the one-hot Choice target. This is a change
of target with no new parameters. On held-out urgency it takes NLL from 2.07 to 1.23 at flat accuracy
(.505 to .508), and on JevBench's 12 ordinal items it changes no decision (\Cref{sec:results:typed}).

\textbf{Noul} uses a dedicated Bernoulli head on a query-only suffix that renders no candidate text:
\[
  P(\text{yes}) = \sigma(w_n^{\top} \hD),
\]
with the complement assigned to ``no''. Since no candidate enters the computation, reversing the label
order cannot change $P(\text{yes})$; a two-way Choice over the same labels moves it by up to .55 under
reversal (\Cref{sec:results:typed}). The abstention gate applies to Noul unchanged.

\textbf{Per-row routing.} A row takes the Bernoulli path if and only if its candidate set is exactly
$\{\text{yes}, \text{no}\}$, and Score rows take the ordinal target; every other row is scored by the
Choice path, bit-identical to a model with the typed heads disabled. Routing is a property of the row
because a batch mixes types: an early build that applied the Bernoulli head to every row scored Choice
and Score rows with no candidate text and fell to chance on them (\Cref{app:typed-primitives}).

\subsection{Factored abstention gate}
\label{sec:method:null}

Abstention is a separate gate with its own inputs, outside the candidate softmax. Given scores
$s_1,\dots,s_\numcands$ from \Cref{eq:readout}, the gate reads a vector $z$ of permutation-invariant
statistics over the valid candidates (maximum score, top-2 margin, mean score, log-mean-exp of the
scores), the projected decision state, and the mean and variance of the projected candidate states
$v_j$, and computes $r = \sigma(g_\nullsym(z))$. The returned distribution is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:factored-null}
  P(\cand{j} \mid \state, \query, \cands) = (1-r)\,\frac{\exp(s_j/T)}{\sum_{k=1}^{\numcands}\exp(s_k/T)},
  \qquad
  \pnull = r,
\end{equation}
implemented as logits $\ell_j = \log \mathrm{softmax}(s/T)_j + \log(1-r)$ and $\ell_\nullsym = \log r$,
so a standard cross-entropy over $\numcands + 1$ outcomes trains both parts. Two properties follow
exactly from the construction.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Pairwise non-null odds are invariant to abstention.} For fixed scores,
  $P(\cand{j})/P(\cand{k}) = \exp((s_j - s_k)/T)$, independent of $r$: the multiplier $(1-r)$ is common to
  every non-null candidate, so abstention never reorders or reweights the candidates among themselves.
  The contextual scores $s_j$ are themselves computed in the context of the whole set and remain
  set-dependent.
  \item \emph{The gate is temperature-invariant.} $T$ enters only the candidate softmax and $r$ is computed
  from untempered statistics, so recalibrating $T$ sharpens or flattens the candidate distribution
  without moving $\pnull$.
\end{enumerate}
Together with the exact order invariance of the Bernoulli head (\Cref{sec:method:typed}), these are the
structural guarantees of the output layer. The gate is also a symmetric function of the candidate set:
every input to $z$ is invariant to permuting the pairs $(s_j, v_j)$, so it adds no dependence on
candidate order beyond what the contextual scores carry. Whether to abstain depends on what was offered,
so $r$ is set-dependent by design. The null is never
rendered as a candidate line. The matched experiment in \Cref{sec:analysis:null} removes the rendered
``none of the above'' line from a model that already has this gate and shows why: a rendered null
competes for softmax mass that dilutes as $\numcands$ grows, and removing it takes MMLU-Pro false
abstention from .692 to .003.

\subsection{Cost model}
\label{sec:method:cost}

$M$ decisions against one state cost one prefix pass and $M$ suffix passes,
\[
  O(|\state|) \;+\; M \cdot O(|\query| + |\cands|)
\]
token positions, where $|\cands|$ counts the rendered candidate tokens. Each suffix position attends to
the cached state, and every position, in the prefix and in the suffix, runs through only the kept
71\% of the layers. The state term is paid once and amortized over every question asked of it; the
per-decision term grows with the length of the question and the candidate text, and is independent of
the other questions. On one H100 in our single-stream research harness, a decision at $\numcands = 2$
costs 45, 57 and 59\,ms at 1.7B, 4B and 14B, so an $8\times$ larger backbone is $1.3\times$ slower.
At $\numcands = 256$ the candidate term dominates and grows with the backbone: the marginal per-query cost
in a batch of 32 is 28.0\,ms at 1.7B and 109.7\,ms at 14B, against 2.7 and 3.9\,ms at $\numcands = 2$.
For large candidate sets a candidate-blind scorer, whose cost does not depend on rendering candidates
through the backbone, shortlists to a bounded set for the contextual readout; \Cref{sec:results:cost}
gives the full sweep and the crossover.
