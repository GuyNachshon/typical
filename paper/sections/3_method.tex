\section{Typical}
\label{sec:method}

Typical turns a pretrained causal language model into a decision function whose output is read from hidden
states in one pass over a short suffix.

\subsection{The decision pass}
\label{sec:method:task}

\begin{definition}[Typed decision]
\label{def:task}
A decision is a tuple $\decision = (\state, \query, \cands, \dtype)$: a state $\state$ (the document,
transcript or trace the decision is about), a question $\query$ (the instruction or rubric), a candidate
set $\cands = \{\cand{1},\dots,\cand{\numcands}\}$ of label strings supplied at request time, and a type
$\dtype \in \{\textsc{Choice}, \textsc{Score}, \textsc{Noul}\}$. The model returns
\[
  P(\cand{j} \mid \state, \query, \cands),\ j = 1,\dots,\numcands,
  \qquad
  \pnull = P(\nullsym \mid \state, \query, \cands),
  \qquad
  \pnull + \textstyle\sum_{j} P(\cand{j} \mid \state, \query, \cands) = 1,
\]
and emits no token.
\end{definition}

The abstention event $\nullsym$ is available under every type and is never one of the candidates.
Because $\cands$ is text supplied per call, the model has no output index for any label and must read the
candidates to score them.

\paragraph{Cached state and causal suffix.}
\label{sec:method:pass}
Typical encodes the state once as an ordinary causal prefix and retains its key-value cache. Each question
is a short causal suffix: the question text, the candidates rendered as lettered lines, an answer cue, and
a terminal decision token. A decision is therefore a forward pass on $\mathrm{concat}(\state, \query,
\cands)$ with the state half read from cache (\Cref{app:cache-equivalence}).

\paragraph{Contextual-candidate readout.}
\label{sec:method:readout}
At the tap layer the suffix pass yields the terminal decision state $\hD$, the hidden state at the
decision token, and for each candidate a contextual state $\hctx{j}$, the mean hidden state over the
tokens of candidate $j$'s rendered span. Candidate $j$ receives the score
\begin{equation}
  u = W_h \hD, \qquad v_j = W_c\, \hctx{j}, \qquad
  s_j = \frac{u^{\top} v_j}{\sqrt{d_v}} + w^{\top}(u \odot v_j) + g(u \odot v_j),
  \label{eq:readout}
\end{equation}
where $W_h$ and $W_c$ are layer-normalized linear projections to width $d_v$, $w$ is a vector and $g$ a
two-layer MLP. The option letters stay in the rendered text and are never read out. Because the
candidates are tokens in the same causal computation as the state and the question, $\hctx{j}$ carries the
candidate's meaning together with its slot and its set, and $\hD$ has attended to all of them.
\Cref{sec:analysis:readout} compares \emph{N1}, the letter-logit readout (the next-token logits of the
option letters at $\hD$, with a softmax null), \emph{N2}, the candidate-blind semantic readout
(\Cref{eq:readout} on a frozen text embedding of each candidate), and \emph{N3}, the contextual-candidate
readout Typical deploys (\Cref{eq:readout} on $\hctx{j}$); \Cref{app:method-detail} lists what each pair
shares.

\paragraph{Mid-depth tap.}
\label{sec:method:depth}
We truncate the backbone at about 71\% of its depth: the readout taps layer 20 of 28 at 1.7B, 26 of 36 at
4B and 8B, and 28 of 40 at 14B, and the layers above the tap are never computed. LoRA \citep{hu2022lora}
at rank 16 adapts the top eight kept layers, and everything below is frozen. The tap carries evidence and
question-conditioned knowledge in one model, where the final layer does not (\Cref{sec:analysis:depth}),
and every released and ladder model uses it.

\paragraph{Cost.}
\label{sec:method:cost}
$M$ decisions against one state cost $O(|\state|) + M \cdot O(|\query| + |\cands|)$ token positions, where
$|\cands|$ counts the rendered candidate tokens, and every position runs through only the kept 71\% of the
layers. The state term is paid once; the per-decision term grows with the question and the candidate text
and is independent of the other questions. At large $\numcands$ the rendered candidates dominate it, and
in throughput mode a candidate-blind scorer is cheaper per query by a margin that grows with $\numcands$
(\Cref{sec:results:cost}).

\subsection{Typed outputs and abstention}
\label{sec:method:typed}

The type is a contract on the returned distribution, enforced by the head and the training target. All
three types share the backbone and the tap. \emph{Choice} is categorical over $\cands$: the
$\numcands$-way readout of \Cref{eq:readout}, trained with cross-entropy against the gold distribution
over $\cands \cup \{\nullsym\}$ through the gate below. \emph{Score} is categorical over ordered levels,
where near misses cost less than distant ones: it uses the Choice readout with an ordinal-smoothed target
$\tilde{t}_j \propto \exp(-|j - y|/\tauord)$ for gold level $y$, normalized over the valid levels, with
$\tauord = 0.7$; $\tauord \to 0$ recovers the one-hot Choice target, and the change adds no parameters.
\emph{Noul} is a yes/no decision with $\cands = \{\text{yes}, \text{no}\}$: a dedicated Bernoulli head,
$P(\text{yes}) = \sigma(w_n^{\top} \hD)$, on a query-only suffix that renders no candidate text, with the
complement assigned to ``no'' and its own label-symmetric gate, so the returned yes, no and $\nullsym$
probabilities are exactly invariant to label order. Routing is per row, because a batch mixes types; rows
that are not Noul or Score are scored by the Choice path, bit-identical to a model with the typed heads
disabled (\Cref{app:method-detail,app:typed-primitives}).

\paragraph{Factored abstention gate.}
\label{sec:method:null}
Abstention is a separate gate outside the candidate softmax. The gate reads permutation-invariant
statistics of the scores $s_j$, the projected decision state and the moments of the projected candidate
states $v_j$ (\Cref{app:method-detail}), and computes $r = \sigma(g_\nullsym(z))$. The returned
distribution is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:factored-null}
  P(\cand{j} \mid \state, \query, \cands) = (1-r)\,\frac{\exp(s_j/T)}{\sum_{k=1}^{\numcands}\exp(s_k/T)},
  \qquad
  \pnull = r,
\end{equation}
trained by one cross-entropy over $\numcands + 1$ outcomes. Two properties follow exactly.
(i)~\emph{Pairwise non-null odds are invariant to abstention}: for fixed scores
$P(\cand{j})/P(\cand{k}) = \exp((s_j - s_k)/T)$, independent of $r$; the contextual scores themselves
remain set-dependent. (ii)~\emph{The gate is temperature-invariant}: $T$ enters only the candidate softmax
and $r$ reads untempered statistics, so recalibrating $T$ never moves $\pnull$. The gate adds no order
dependence, while $r$ depends on what was offered by design. We never render the null as a candidate
line; \Cref{sec:analysis:null} removes that line from a model that already has the gate and measures the
difference.

\subsection{Training}
\label{sec:train}

Every row is a typed decision rendered as above and trained with cross-entropy against a hard or soft
gold distribution over $\cands \cup \{\nullsym\}$, plus distillation at 14B, using AdamW in bf16 at batch
size 64 for 8{,}000--12{,}000 steps (\Cref{app:hyperparams}). \Cref{tab:corpora} lists the seven corpora,
and \Cref{sec:results} measures the six recipe choices below.

\paragraph{Family-balanced mixture.}
\label{sec:train:mixture}
We draw batches by decision family, so no large corpus dominates by row count: $E$ is evidence and
wide-vocabulary classification, $K$ closed-book multiple-choice knowledge, $W$ rubric-conditioned
workflow decisions, and $U$ soft-target uncertainty (sources in \Cref{app:method-detail}). The released
recipe samples $E{:}0.40$, $K{:}0.15$, $W{:}0.35$, $U{:}0.10$.

\paragraph{Counterfactual rubric groups.}
\label{sec:train:rubric}
DecisionMix v2 (\texttt{data\_wh}) is a programmatic rule-engine corpus in which every row belongs to a
\emph{counterfactual rubric group}: the same state and candidate set under at least two rubrics with
distinct gold answers. A predictor that ignores the rubric assigns one distribution to every member of a
group, so on a two-member group with golds $y_1 \neq y_2$ it incurs an average cross-entropy of at least
$\log 2$ nats, and the loss falls below that floor only by reading the rubric. Held-out compositions,
domains, styles and grammars are evaluation-only (\Cref{app:corpora}).

\paragraph{Soft-target uncertainty.}
\label{sec:train:uncertainty}
Decisions with a single correct answer cannot teach a model how much probability to spread. The
uncertainty corpus \texttt{data\_u} draws on UNLI \citep{chen2020unli}, the ambiguous rows of AmbiEnt,
and four synthetic generators with exact closed-form posteriors (\Cref{tab:app-u}); ChaosNLI
\citep{nie2020chaosnli} is evaluation-only.

\paragraph{Null augmentation.}
\label{sec:train:nullaug}
A copy of 20\% of $W$ rows drops the gold candidate and any rendered catch-all option and takes $\nullsym$
as its target. Under 1\% of $W$ rows carry a null target against over 20\% of $E$ rows, so a model trained
on both learns two operating points that no single post-hoc temperature and null offset can serve.

\paragraph{Evidence-first long states.}
\label{sec:train:long}
Training right-truncates states at the window, so rendering the case facts after the policy body in the
long-state corpus \texttt{data\_wf\_long} lost the facts from most rows (\Cref{app:long-state-stats}). The
fixed recipe renders facts first and drops any row longer than the window. The v2 releases train at a
2{,}048-token window and the 14B model at 3{,}072.

\paragraph{Selection on held-out likelihood.}
\label{sec:train:selection}
Selection on in-distribution loss favors the hard-label majority of the mixture over the soft and ordinal
minority, so the v2 releases and the 14B model select checkpoints on held-out negative log-likelihood over
the uncertainty and curriculum validation sets; the v1 releases do not. The 14B model also distills from
its own frozen backbone (\Cref{app:hyperparams}). Selection enters the 14B recipe together with
evidence-first long states, the 3{,}072-token window, DecisionMix v2, the uncertainty corpus and the typed
heads, and is never isolated.
