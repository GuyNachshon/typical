\section{Training decision models}
\label{sec:train}

Every row is a typed decision rendered as in \Cref{sec:method:pass} and trained with cross-entropy
against a hard or soft gold distribution over $\cands \cup \{\nullsym\}$, plus distillation at 14B,
using AdamW in bf16 at batch size 64 for 8{,}000--12{,}000 steps (\Cref{app:hyperparams}).
\Cref{tab:corpora} (\Cref{app:training-corpora}) lists the seven corpora, and \Cref{sec:results}
measures the six recipe choices below.

\subsection{Family-balanced mixture}
\label{sec:train:mixture}

Batches are drawn by decision family, so no large corpus dominates by row count. $E$ is evidence and
classification (SNLI \citep{bowman2015snli}, MNLI \citep{williams2018mnli}, ANLI \citep{nie2020anli},
BoolQ \citep{clark2019boolq}, and wide-vocabulary intent and topic sets including CLINC-150, Banking77 and
HWU64 \citep{larson2019clinc,casanueva2020banking77}), spanning many label vocabularies at few rows per
source so that the model must read the rendered candidates. $K$ is closed-book multiple-choice knowledge
from nine sources, with TruthfulQA-MC1 \citep{lin2022truthfulqa} held out and a leakage audit against
MMLU-Pro \citep{wang2024mmlupro,hendrycks2021mmlu}. $W$ is rubric-conditioned workflow decisions (routing,
eligibility, extraction, urgency, tool selection, long policy documents), and $U$ is soft-target
uncertainty. The released recipe samples $E{:}0.40$, $K{:}0.15$, $W{:}0.35$, $U{:}0.10$. Unbalanced workflow data costs
evidence accuracy, mostly through false abstention; with the $U$ share and null augmentation, $E{:}0.40$ keeps
evidence within about a point of the evidence-only base, where the sweep of \Cref{sec:results:mixture},
run without either, needs $E \ge .45$.

\subsection{Counterfactual rubric groups}
\label{sec:train:rubric}

A workflow model that learns ``this kind of ticket gets that label'' fails the first time the policy
changes. DecisionMix v2 (\texttt{data\_wh}) is a programmatic rule-engine corpus of 60{,}605 rows over 12
domains and six boolean conditions at compositional depths 1--6, in which every row belongs to a
\emph{counterfactual rubric group}: the same state and candidate set under at least two rubrics with
distinct gold answers. A predictor that ignores the rubric assigns one distribution to every member of a
group, so on a two-member group with golds $y_1 \neq y_2$ it incurs an average cross-entropy of at least
$\log 2$ nats, and the loss falls below that floor only by reading the rubric. Depth-7 compositions,
three whole domains, two rubric styles and one grammar per level are evaluation-only (\Cref{app:corpora}).

\subsection{Soft-target uncertainty corpus}
\label{sec:train:uncertainty}

Decisions with a single correct answer cannot teach a model how much probability to spread. The
uncertainty corpus \texttt{data\_u} (31{,}029 rows, all soft targets) draws on the UNLI validation split
\citep{chen2020unli}, the ambiguous rows of AmbiEnt, and four synthetic generators with exact closed-form
posteriors, each holding out a parameter range (\Cref{tab:app-u}); ChaosNLI \citep{nie2020chaosnli} is
evaluation-only.

\subsection{Null augmentation}
\label{sec:train:nullaug}

A copy of 20\% of $W$ rows drops the gold candidate and any rendered catch-all option and takes $\nullsym$
as its target. The fix belongs in the data because the families disagree about abstention: under 1\% of
$W$ rows carry a null target against over 20\% of $E$ rows, so a model trained on both learns two
operating points that no single post-hoc temperature and null offset can serve
(\Cref{sec:results:mixture,sec:results:calibration}).

\subsection{Evidence-first long states}
\label{sec:train:long}

The long-state corpus \texttt{data\_wf\_long} (23{,}318 multi-paragraph policy documents) was first
rendered with the case facts after the policy body, and training right-truncates states at the window, so
at 1{,}024 tokens 98.8\% of rows lost their facts (\Cref{app:long-state-stats}). The fixed recipe renders
facts first and drops any row longer than the window. The v2 releases train at a 2{,}048-token window and
the 14B model at 3{,}072; \Cref{sec:results:long} isolates the effect with a render-crossed design.

\subsection{Selection on held-out likelihood}
\label{sec:train:selection}

Selection on in-distribution loss favors the hard-label majority of the mixture over the soft and ordinal
minority, so the v2 releases and the 14B model select checkpoints on held-out negative log-likelihood over
the uncertainty and curriculum validation sets; the v1 releases do not. The 14B model also distills from
its own frozen backbone under a fixed three-shot letter rendering, $\alpha \cdot \mathrm{CE} + \beta \cdot
\mathrm{KL}$ at $T = 2$ on 63k labelled rows, and a 14B run without distillation reaches the same held-out
Score NLL. Selection enters the 14B recipe together with evidence-first long states, the 3{,}072-token
window, DecisionMix v2, the uncertainty corpus and the typed heads, and is never isolated;
\Cref{sec:results:calibration} reports what the bundle does.
