\subsection{Method and recipe detail}
\label{app:method-detail}

\paragraph{Suffix batching.} A batch of suffixes against one cached state runs in chunks, and the suffixes
do not attend to one another.

\paragraph{Readouts.} \Cref{eq:readout} is a bilinear form with a small learned correction on the
elementwise product. N1, the standard multiple-choice interface read without generating, scores $\hD$
against the output embeddings of the option letters. N2 embeds the candidate text with a frozen
text-embedding model, which carries meaning and no slot identity. N2 and N3 share the scorer, the
factored null and the feature normalization and differ only in the candidate vector. N1 and N3 differ in
the readout and in the null form native to each; option shuffling during training is shared by all three.

\paragraph{Gate inputs.} The Choice gate reads a vector $z$ of permutation-invariant statistics over the
valid candidates (maximum score, top-2 margin, mean score, log-mean-exp of the scores), the projected
decision state, and the mean and variance of the projected candidate states $v_j$. It is symmetric in the
pairs $(s_j, v_j)$. \Cref{eq:factored-null} is implemented as logits $\ell_j = \log
\mathrm{softmax}(s/T)_j + \log(1-r)$ and $\ell_\nullsym = \log r$, so one cross-entropy over
$\numcands + 1$ outcomes trains both parts, and property (i) holds because $(1-r)$ multiplies every
non-null candidate. A null rendered as a candidate line would compete for softmax mass that dilutes as
$\numcands$ grows. The Noul gate has the form of \Cref{eq:factored-null} with its own parameters; its
score statistics come from $\log P(\text{yes})$ and $\log P(\text{no})$ and its state input is $\hD$,
since no candidate span is rendered. Every Noul gate input is symmetric in the two labels.

\paragraph{Routing.} A row takes the Bernoulli path if and only if its candidate set is exactly
$\{\text{yes}, \text{no}\}$, Score rows take the ordinal target, and every other row is scored by the
Choice path.

\paragraph{Cost.} The candidate-blind scorer's per-decision cost does not grow with the rendered candidate
set.

\paragraph{Mixture sources.} $E$ is SNLI \citep{bowman2015snli}, MNLI \citep{williams2018mnli}, ANLI
\citep{nie2020anli}, BoolQ \citep{clark2019boolq}, and wide-vocabulary intent and topic sets including
CLINC-150, Banking77 and HWU64 \citep{larson2019clinc,casanueva2020banking77}, spanning many label
vocabularies at few rows per source so that the model must read the rendered candidates. $K$ draws on nine
sources, with TruthfulQA-MC1 \citep{lin2022truthfulqa} held out and a leakage audit against MMLU-Pro
\citep{wang2024mmlupro,hendrycks2021mmlu}. $W$ covers routing, eligibility, extraction, urgency, tool
selection and long policy documents. The sampler weights and the sweep behind them are in
\Cref{sec:results:mixture}.

\paragraph{Recipe corpora.} DecisionMix v2 has 60{,}605 rows over 12 domains and six boolean conditions at
compositional depths 1--6; depth-7 compositions, three whole domains, two rubric styles and one grammar
per level are evaluation-only. \texttt{data\_u} has 31{,}029 rows, all soft targets, uses the UNLI
validation split, and each of its four generators holds out a parameter range. \texttt{data\_wf\_long}
has 23{,}318 multi-paragraph policy documents (\Cref{app:long-state-stats}); \Cref{sec:results:long}
isolates the evidence-first render with a render-crossed design. Null augmentation and the mixture act on
the operating points measured in \Cref{sec:results:mixture,sec:results:calibration}, and
\Cref{sec:results:calibration} reports what the 14B selection bundle does.

\subsection{Cached-state equivalence}
\label{app:cache-equivalence}

An equivalence test on the real model checks that the cached-state pass of \Cref{sec:method:pass} computes
the same decision as a forward pass on $\mathrm{concat}(\state, \query, \cands)$. Cached-state decisions
match the full-concatenation path to within $10^{-3}$ across suffix chunk sizes 1, 2, 3, 5 and 64, are
consistent under permutation of the queries in a batch, and repeat exactly.

\subsection{Long-state corpus statistics}
\label{app:long-state-stats}

States in \texttt{data\_wf\_long} have p10/p50/p90 lengths of 1{,}190, 1{,}845 and 2{,}490 tokens. In the
original render, with the case facts after the policy body, right-truncation at a 1{,}024-token window
removed the facts from 98.8\% of rows, and at 256 tokens from all of them. The facts-first render places
the case inside the first 8\% of the text, and \texttt{--drop\_truncated} removes any row longer than the
training window.
