\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Decisions live in candidate-contextual states at mid-depth.} Three measurements point at the
same place. Question-conditioned capability appears only when the candidates are processed inside the
language model's own forward pass (\Cref{sec:analysis:deltaq}); among readouts that do process them,
the candidate's contextual state carries the decision and an independently embedded copy of the same
text does not (\Cref{sec:analysis:readout}); and the layer that holds it sits before the top of the
stack specializes for next-token prediction (\Cref{sec:analysis:depth}). This refines the cross-encoder
versus dual-encoder picture. In every configuration we tested, a candidate-independent state reproduced the teacher's candidate
priors and calibration, and the question-dependent component appeared only with the candidates in
context. The reusable-state formulation remains valuable for shortlisting large
candidate sets, a sub-task that runs on priors (\Cref{sec:results:cost}), and $\dq$ is the measurement
that tells the two roles apart. We recommend reporting it for any architecture that scores candidates
against a precomputed representation.

\paragraph{An output type is a contract, and structure enforces it.} Each typed primitive fixes a
property that post-hoc calibration cannot reach. The Bernoulli head is order-invariant because there is
no candidate order to be sensitive to; the ordinal target moves probability mass toward adjacent levels
without changing a decision; the gate keeps null AUROC between .92 and .98 from
$\numcands = 5$ to $\numcands = 150$, where a rendered abstain option collapsed at both ends. The same lesson appears in reverse at the output layer: one global
temperature and null offset, fit on held-out soft targets, wants opposite corrections for an
evidence-trained and a workflow-trained checkpoint (\Cref{sec:results:calibration}). Probability
quality for a decision model is indexed by decision type, and the productive place to shape it is the
head, the target and the training data.

\paragraph{The distribution of decision types is a first-order lever.} Several of the largest effects in
this paper come from data and selection with the architecture held fixed: counterfactual rubric groups
(35--40 points on held-out families, grammars and styles), null augmentation (2.6--6.0 points on
label-space accuracy, from teaching the model when to abstain), family-balanced sampling (bringing
evidence and intent accuracy back to within 1 and 3--4 points of the evidence-only base after an
unbalanced mix cost 10--11), evidence-first rendering of long states (11.2 points), and a 14B recipe built around calibration-based
checkpoint selection (a threefold reduction in held-out ordinal NLL). Decision models are trained on a mixture of decision \emph{shapes}, and each shape the
mixture contains is one the model learns.

\paragraph{A benchmark score measures capability together with interface compatibility.} Changing only
the rendering moves a frozen 9B model 12.5 points on the same items (\Cref{sec:results:rendering}), more
than the gap between many systems a benchmark is used to rank. Two practices made our own measurements
decisive. The first is to cross training render with evaluation render, so that data effects and
compatibility effects separate. The second is to build an evaluation set with the power to resolve the
effect in question: 605 generated, leak-checked long states settled at $p < 10^{-9}$ a question that a
19-item benchmark cell returned as $p = .232$. Pre-registration protects a decision rule only when the
metric it names can resolve the effect it gates.

\paragraph{What a single pass computes.} Typical decides in one forward pass of fixed depth, with no
intermediate tokens. Fixed-depth transformers correspond to constant-depth threshold circuits
\citep{merrill2023parallelism}, while conditioning on self-generated intermediate tokens extends what
they can compute \citep{merrill2024expressive,feng2023chain}. The decisions that remain hard for every
model we trained, temporal arithmetic, unit conversion, expected value and multi-attribute trade-offs
(\Cref{app:hard-tier}), have the serial shape that picture singles out. That makes a concrete next step:
keep the typed readout and the cached state, and add a bounded amount of intermediate computation
before the decision token, in the form of extra passes or a short scratchpad. The interface in this
paper is the natural terminal layer for such a system, since a decision still has to end in a typed,
thresholdable distribution over the options the caller supplied.
