Software that calls a language model to make a decision -- route this ticket, flag this transaction,
score this alert -- typically gets an answer by prompting the model to generate text and parsing the
result: slow, sensitive to answer surface form, and poorly calibrated. We first report a negative result
that motivates our design: a decision state computed \emph{before} the candidate set is known transfers a
teacher's candidate priors and calibration but never its question-conditioned knowledge, under six tested
factorizations and objectives and at every backbone depth, shown with a control that swaps the real
question for a shuffled one. This rules out the most appealing candidate-blind architecture and motivates
Typical, which instead renders the query and its runtime-defined candidates inside one causal suffix
against a state cached once, reading calibrated, typed decisions with no generated tokens. A direct
readout scores the terminal decision state against each candidate's own contextual hidden state --
carrying slot identity a candidate-blind embedding cannot -- recovering a letter-generating interface's
question-conditioned knowledge with a quarter of its order sensitivity; a null probability comes from a
gate over permutation-invariant candidate-score statistics rather than competing for softmax mass as a
rendered abstain option. The same readout supports three typed primitives -- Choice, an ordinal-smoothed
Score, and an order-invariant Bernoulli Noul -- by changing only the target distribution and terminal
head. Scaled across Qwen3 (1.7B/4B/8B/14B) and once on Qwen3.5-4B with matched frozen controls, the recipe
reaches .931 standard-tier accuracy on the public subset of JevBench, a third-party typed-decision
benchmark, at 14B (.861 at Qwen3.5-4B) at 15--60\,ms per decision on our single-stream harness, and
calibration-shaped checkpoint selection with frozen-teacher distillation takes held-out soft-target
negative log-likelihood from 2.87 to 0.95 at the same size. Against these gains, we report the honest
limit: a harder tier of compositional, multi-step decisions stays at or near chance for every checkpoint
we trained, including a same-size frozen backbone read with a few exemplars and no training, which still
beats our best trained model there -- a comparison we report with its own confound, since state/option
rendering alone moves a frozen backbone's score by a similar margin.
