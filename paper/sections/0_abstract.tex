Software that asks a language model for a decision needs a probability for each option it supplies,
and a way to say that none of them fit. Typical reads that distribution directly from a pretrained
language model's hidden states, without generating any text. The model encodes the input document once
into a key-value cache, reads each question with its candidate options in a short suffix, and scores
every candidate with a small head at 71\% of the network's depth. Outputs are typed: a choice among
options, an ordinal score, a yes/no probability, and a separate probability of abstaining. Three
measurements fix the design. In every configuration we tested, the model's question-dependent knowledge
appeared only when the candidates were read inside the forward pass. Scoring each candidate's in-context
representation matches the standard answer-letter readout on that knowledge, with a quarter of its
sensitivity to option edits. Abstention belongs in a separate gate:
also listing ``none of the above'' among the options raises MMLU-Pro false abstention from .003 to .692. Training on
counterfactual rubrics, where only the rule decides the answer, improves held-out rule families by
35--40 points. A decision takes 45\,ms at 1.7B and 60\,ms at 14B parameters, and the 14B model reaches
.931 on the JevBench standard tier. We release 1.7B and 4B models and an inference package.
