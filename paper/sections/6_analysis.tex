\section{Where the decision lives}
\label{sec:analysis}

This section locates the decision inside the backbone. It compares readouts of one decision pass
(\Cref{sec:analysis:readout}), tests decision states computed before the candidates are known
(\Cref{sec:analysis:deltaq}), varies the layer the head reads (\Cref{sec:analysis:depth}), and isolates
how abstention enters the computation (\Cref{sec:analysis:null}). Unless stated otherwise, the models are
1.7B, trained for 12k steps on the evidence and knowledge mixture, and $\dqsh$ is measured on the
1{,}200-item MMLU-Pro probe against the teacher's .102.

\subsection{The contextual-candidate readout}
\label{sec:analysis:readout}

\textbf{Scoring the decision state against each candidate's contextual state keeps the letter-logit
readout's question-conditioned signal within seed noise, with a quarter of its pairwise log-odds shift
and 2--7 more points on natural language inference.} \Cref{tab:n1n3} compares the three readouts of
\Cref{sec:method:readout} on the same pass: N1 reads the next-token logits of the option letters, N2
scores $\hD$ against an independently computed embedding of each option's text, and N3 scores $\hD$
against each option's contextual state $\hctx{j}$. All arms render the candidates in the suffix, train
on identical data with per-step option shuffling, and read the full 28-layer backbone; N1 and N3 each
have two seeds.

\textbf{The candidate's contextual state carries the decision.} N2 runs the candidates through the
language model and scores the same $\hD$ with the same scorer; it differs from N3 only in scoring
against an independent embedding of the option text. It loses 13 points of among-$\numcands$ accuracy
against N1 (.198 vs.\ .325), half of the question-conditioned signal ($\dqsh$ .058 vs.\ .114), and
collapses on Banking77 at $\numcands{=}77$ (.012 against .531 and .497 for N1 and N3). The pattern fits
a decision state that encodes which rendered slot holds the answer: the letter embedding and the
contextual state both carry slot identity, and an independently embedded label carries only its meaning.

\textbf{N3 matches N1 on question dependence and is far less sensitive to the candidate set.} Across
seeds N1 averages $\dqsh = .109$ and N3 .106, against the teacher's .102; the seed-to-seed spread is
.01--.02 for both readouts, so the two are within seed noise of each other and of the teacher.
Among-$\numcands$ accuracy is 1--2 points lower for N3 (.314/.301 vs.\ .325/.318). Adding three unrelated
options shifts N3's pairwise log-odds by .09--.13 against .44--.46 for N1, a quarter of the shift, and
reordering moves N3's probabilities by .10--.11 against .165. On evidence tasks N3 leads in both seeds
by 2 points on SNLI, 6--7 on MNLI and 4--5 on ANLI, and trails by 2--3 on BoolQ. Per-step option
shuffling is shared by both arms, so the difference in order sensitivity is the readout's. N3 is the
readout Typical deploys; its Choice output is order-robust, and exact order invariance is the Bernoulli
head's contract (\Cref{sec:results:typed}).

\subsection{Candidate-blind decision states}
\label{sec:analysis:deltaq}

\textbf{In every configuration we tested, question-conditioned capability appeared only when the
candidates were processed inside the forward pass.} Six candidate-blind variants, spanning three head
factorizations, two distillation objectives and two tap depths, all have $\dqsh$ within .03 of zero,
while the letter-logit and contextual readouts and the teacher, which compute over the candidates in
context, sit at .09--.12 and even the semantic readout N2 keeps .058 (\Cref{fig:deltaq}). A candidate-blind variant computes a decision state $Z(\state, \query)$ before the
candidates are seen and scores each candidate against it with a learned head; each is distilled from the
listwise teacher.

The result holds across every lever we varied (\Cref{tab:blind}). A single decision vector retains as
much as eight probes. Cross-attention from the probes over all candidate tokens leaves $\dqsh$ at
$-.003$ while improving null quality (CLINC out-of-scope accuracy .568 $\to$ .727). Supervising the
change in the teacher's log-odds across seven candidate sets for the same question gives $\dqsh$ of
exactly zero, against $-.023$ for its steps-matched single-set control. Reading the full 28 layers
instead of layer 20 leaves $\dqsh$ at $-.008$, lowers among-$\numcands$ accuracy to .147, and regresses
the evidence tasks (SNLI/MNLI/ANLI/BoolQ .881/.807/.462/.712 against .905/.873/.541/.826 at layer 20;
\Cref{tab:app-blind-closure}). Two variants fall just outside the $\pm.02$ band, in opposite directions
(\texttt{z1} $+.023$, \texttt{zr} $-.026$), which is the spread 1{,}200 items allow; every variant is
separated from the teacher's .102. The one mechanistic alternative the data leave open is an optimization
failure in the set-conditioning path, whose zero-initialized cross-attention stayed inert through 4k
steps of direct supervision.

\textbf{What a candidate-blind state does learn explains why accuracy hides the gap.} The variants
inherit the teacher's candidate priors and its calibration. Their accuracy of .12--.17 sits above the
.10 chance rate of ten-option questions and comes from which option strings tend to be correct together;
ECE on the knowledge probe falls from .39 for an earlier baseline to .04; and they are exactly set-stable
(reorder $\Delta p$ and pairwise $\Delta$log-odds both zero) where the teacher is not. On accuracy and
calibration alone the factorization looks sound. $\dqsh$ shows that the question-dependent component
lives in candidate-contextual computation, which sets the design principle for Typical: render the
candidates inside the backbone's forward pass. The candidate-blind state keeps one role, shortlisting
large candidate sets, a task that runs on priors (\Cref{sec:results:cost}).

\subsection{Mid-depth readout}
\label{sec:analysis:depth}

\textbf{Reading at 71\% of depth gives one model that carries both evidence reasoning and
question-conditioned knowledge.} The first full-scale model in this project read the backbone's last
layer and scored 58.6 on SNLI, the hypothesis-only baseline: it predicted entailment without using the
premise. Moving the tap to layer 20 of 28 lifted it to 65.3 frozen and 68.2 with LoRA. The same effect
appears in the contextual readout under a controlled comparison. With head, data and steps fixed,
reading N3 at layer 20 instead of 28 raises SNLI/MNLI/ANLI/BoolQ from .863/.752/.431/.737 to
.906/.865/.519/.834, CLINC-150 from .740 to .862, and lowers validation NLL from .430 to .341, while
$\dqsh$ stays at .118 against .117 at full depth. Evidence accuracy lands within 1--3 points of a
candidate-blind energy-scoring baseline on every evidence set, and 8 points above it on CLINC-150, so
the evidence and knowledge regimes that had needed separate models are served by one. For a
candidate-blind state the same change in depth moves nothing (\Cref{sec:analysis:deltaq}).

The final layers of a causal language model specialize for next-token prediction, and that the most
useful features for a downstream decision sit below them agrees with work on layer pruning and probing
\citep{gromov2024deeperlayers,men2024shortgpt,tenney2019pipeline,belrose2023tunedlens}. For a deployed
decision interface the choice is decisive and interacts with the readout: depth recovers evidence for
the candidate-aware readout and leaves a candidate-blind state unchanged. Every checkpoint we report
uses the 71\% tap, ported across sizes as layer 20 of 28, 26 of 36 and 28 of 40.

\subsection{Abstention as a gate}
\label{sec:analysis:null}

\textbf{Abstention must be a gate and never a rendered candidate.} A rendered ``none of the above'' line
competes for softmax mass with the real candidates, so its share depends on how many there are. We
isolate this with a matched pair: the same model (N3 at layer 20, with the factored gate of
\Cref{eq:factored-null}) with and without a rendered $\nullsym$ line in the suffix. With the line, the
abstention behavior is a function of $\numcands$: on CLINC-150 with the gold label absent the model
abstains with probability .02 at $\numcands{=}5$ and 1.00 at $\numcands{=}150$, where it abstains on
every item, and on Banking77 at $\numcands{=}77$ null AUROC is .00 by the same mechanism.

Removing the line removes both degenerate endpoints and makes the $\numcands$-sweep smooth: null AUROC on CLINC-150 is
.98/.95/.92/.95 at $\numcands = 5/20/50/150$ and on Banking77 .90/.83/.69 at $\numcands = 5/20/77$.
Downstream, MMLU-Pro false abstention falls from .692 to .003 and accuracy rises from .133 to .353, the
KL divergence to the teacher's distribution on MMLU-Pro falls from 2.43 to .27, Banking77 with
all 77 labels rises from .063 to .579, and CLINC out-of-scope accuracy from .639 to .798, with $\dqsh$
unchanged (.118 vs.\ .122) and evidence tasks at the same level (\Cref{tab:app-null-fix}). The
false-abstain change is far outside its seed-to-seed variation of .05--.10. The price is 2--7 points on
intent and topic label spaces (HWU64 .801 $\to$ .757, 20NG .589 $\to$ .515) and a rise in reorder
$\Delta p$ from .077 to .123. The gate's algebraic properties hold by construction
(\Cref{sec:method:null}); this pair shows their empirical consequence, and null augmentation supplies the
training signal that teaches the gate when to fire (\Cref{sec:results:mixture}). This configuration, contextual
readout at 71\% depth with a gate and no rendered $\nullsym$ line, is the architecture of every checkpoint
in \Cref{sec:results}.
