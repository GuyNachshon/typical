\subsection{Evaluation sets}
\label{sec:setup:data}

Training corpora are listed in \Cref{tab:corpora}. We evaluate on evidence and label-space sets (SNLI
\citep{bowman2015snli}, MNLI \citep{williams2018mnli}, ANLI \citep{nie2020anli}, BoolQ

\citep{wang2024mmlupro} scored among the rendered options, with two items matched by a leakage audit
excluded, and TruthfulQA-MC1 \citep{lin2022truthfulqa}; held-out splits of our generators, including the
never-trained DecisionMix level~7 (880 items, uniform-guess rate .441) and ChaosNLI
\citep{nie2020chaosnli}; and 605 held-out long states built for \Cref{sec:results:long}
(\texttt{policy\_permit}, $\numcands{=}2$, majority floor .612; \texttt{action\_select}, $\numcands{=}4$,
floor .233). Six external sets are evaluation-only: \textbf{PagerDuty} (alert triage, yes/no, majority
floor .792), \textbf{jevlogs} (log triage, yes/no, floor .697), \textbf{Mind2Web} (web-agent action
choice, floor .427), \textbf{tree-choice} (taxonomy routing up to $\numcands{=}320$),
\textbf{typed-decisions} (400 cases across all three output types with probabilistic gold) and
\textbf{JevBench} (\Cref{sec:setup:jevbench}). Several are class-imbalanced, so each is reported against
its own majority floor. No training corpus contains any of the 231 public JevBench ids.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\dq$}{Delta-q}: question dependence separated from candidate priors}
\label{sec:setup:deltaq}

A model can clear chance on a multiple-choice set by learning which option strings tend to be correct
together, and accuracy and calibration do not distinguish this from answering the question.

\begin{definition}[Question-dependence gap]
\label{def:deltaq}
For a model $f$ and a set of decisions,
\[
  \dq = \mathrm{Acc}\big(f(\state, \query, \cands)\big) \;-\; \mathrm{Acc}\big(f(\state, \tilde{\query}, \cands)\big),
\]
where $\tilde{\query}$ replaces the real question while the state and the candidate set $\cands$ are held
fixed. In the \emph{choices-only} variant $\tilde{\query}$ is an empty placeholder; in the
\emph{shuffled-question} variant $\dqsh$, primary throughout, it is a real, unrelated question from the
same corpus.
\end{definition}

$\dq$ extends the choices-only control of \citet{balepur2024artifacts} to a shuffled real question, which
is stricter: a degenerate placeholder can leak candidate priors through the rendering, and on one
candidate-blind model the choices-only variant reads $+.017$ where $\dqsh$ reads $.000$. High accuracy
with $\dqsh \approx 0$ means the model answers from the candidate set. The reference is the listwise
teacher, a fine-tuned decoder reading the options in context, with $\dqsh = .102$ on the 1{,}200 MMLU-Pro
items. We treat $|\dqsh| < .02$ as indistinguishable from zero, since .02 is about 1.4 standard errors of
an unpaired difference of proportions at $n{=}1{,}200$ and $p \approx .15$.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:setup:metrics}

We report accuracy, Brier score, expected calibration error (ECE) and negative log-likelihood (NLL)
against hard or soft gold; on soft-gold sets NLL and Brier are primary. \emph{Among-$\numcands$ accuracy}
takes the argmax over candidates and ignores $\pnull$, and the \emph{false-abstain rate} is the fraction of
gold-present items on which $\nullsym$ wins, so abstention and discrimination errors are reported
separately. \emph{Rubric-flip} sets pair rows that share a state and candidate set under two rubrics with
different gold. On MMLU-Pro counterfactual variants, \emph{reorder $\Delta p$} is the largest change in any
candidate's probability when the options are reordered, and \emph{pairwise $\Delta$log-odds} the
absolute shift in log-odds between the two top original candidates when three unrelated options are
added; both are averaged over questions and are zero for a set-invariant scorer.

\subsection{JevBench protocol}
\label{sec:setup:jevbench}

JevBench (\texttt{fstandhartinger/jevbench} v1.2.1) is a third-party benchmark of yes/no, choice
($\numcands{=}2$--6) and ordinal decisions, each item with a state, a rubric and an exact label set. Its
public release has 231 items (72 standard, 48 easy, 111 hard); 146 judge-scored items are private, and
the harness ranks only submissions covering at least 95\% of all items, so every JevBench number here is
on the public ids and unranked. The 72 standard items are 36 states, each in two paraphrases sharing a
\texttt{group} field, so intervals are cluster bootstraps over \texttt{group} (20{,}000 resamples): $\pm6$--13
points on the standard tier and $\pm9$ on the hard tier. Comparisons between our own models use paired
per-item bootstraps. Probabilities are conditioned on non-$\nullsym$: $\pnull$ is dropped and the rest
renormalized. Across all models, option order follows the harness (reversing it moved one checkpoint's
standard accuracy by $\pm4$ points), and majority baselines are .311 standard, .284 easy and .336 hard.
Leaderboard context is in \Cref{app:leaderboard}.

\subsection{Hardware and latency}
\label{sec:setup:hardware}

Models train on single H100 GPUs in bf16 with AdamW, effective batch 64, for 8{,}000--12{,}000 steps.
Latency has two protocols. The \emph{research harness} times one decision in process, one stream, 256-token
state, state encode included and model loading excluded, for $\numcands$ from 2 to 256, with all sizes on
one pod and one PyTorch/CUDA build. The \emph{optimized serving path} is the released inference package,
timed as warm p50 per decision against an already-cached 256-token state at $\numcands{=}2$.
