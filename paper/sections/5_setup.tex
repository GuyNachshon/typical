\section{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Evaluation sets}
\label{sec:setup:data}

Training corpora are in \Cref{tab:corpora}. We evaluate on SNLI \citep{bowman2015snli}, MNLI
\citep{williams2018mnli}, ANLI \citep{nie2020anli}, BoolQ \citep{clark2019boolq}, CLINC-150
\citep{larson2019clinc}, TREC-fine, HWU64, 20 Newsgroups and Banking77 \citep{casanueva2020banking77}
against all 77 labels; a fixed 1{,}200-item MMLU-Pro probe \citep{wang2024mmlupro} scored among the
rendered options (two leakage-matched items excluded) and TruthfulQA-MC1 \citep{lin2022truthfulqa};
held-out generator splits, including the never-trained DecisionMix level~7 (880 items, guess rate .441),
and ChaosNLI \citep{nie2020chaosnli}; and 605 held-out long states (\texttt{policy\_permit},
$\numcands{=}2$, floor .612; \texttt{action\_select}, $\numcands{=}4$, floor .233). Evaluation-only
external sets are PagerDuty (alert triage, yes/no, majority floor .792), jevlogs (log
triage, floor .697), Mind2Web (web-agent actions, floor .427), tree-choice (taxonomy
routing, $\numcands \le 320$), typed-decisions (400 cases of all three types, probabilistic
gold) and JevBench.

\subsection{What ``held out'' means}
\label{sec:setup:heldout}

Each held-out split excludes a whole family, domain, rubric style, grammar or parameter range from
training; none is a random validation split (axes in \Cref{tab:generalization}).

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\dq$}{Delta-q}: question dependence separated from candidate priors}
\label{sec:setup:deltaq}

\begin{definition}[Question-dependence gap]
\label{def:deltaq}
$\dq = \mathrm{Acc}\big(f(\state, \query, \cands)\big) - \mathrm{Acc}\big(f(\state, \tilde{\query},
\cands)\big)$, where $\tilde{\query}$ replaces the real question with the state and candidate set held
fixed: an empty placeholder in the \emph{choices-only} variant, a real unrelated question from the same
corpus in the \emph{shuffled-question} variant $\dqsh$, primary throughout.
\end{definition}

$\dq$ extends the choices-only control of \citet{balepur2024artifacts} to a shuffled real question, which
is stricter: a placeholder can leak candidate priors through the rendering (on one candidate-blind model
choices-only reads $+.017$ where $\dqsh$ reads $.000$). High accuracy with $\dqsh \approx 0$ means the
score is explained by candidate-set priors. The reference is the listwise teacher, a fine-tuned decoder
reading the options in context, at $\dqsh = .102$ on the MMLU-Pro probe. $|\dqsh| < .02$ (about 1.4 standard
errors at $n{=}1{,}200$, $p \approx .15$) counts as zero.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:setup:metrics}

We report accuracy, Brier score, expected calibration error (ECE) and negative log-likelihood (NLL),
with NLL and Brier primary on soft-gold sets. \emph{Among-$\numcands$ accuracy} takes the argmax over
candidates and ignores $\pnull$; the \emph{false-abstain rate} is the fraction of gold-present items on
which $\nullsym$ wins. \emph{Rubric-flip} pairs share a state and candidate set under two rubrics with
different gold. On MMLU-Pro, \emph{reorder $\Delta p$} is the largest change in any candidate's
probability under reordering, and \emph{pairwise $\Delta$log-odds} the shift in log-odds between the two
top candidates when three unrelated options are added; both are zero for a set-invariant scorer.

\subsection{JevBench protocol}
\label{sec:setup:jevbench}

JevBench (\texttt{fstandhartinger/jevbench} v1.2.1) is a third-party benchmark of yes/no, choice
($\numcands{=}2$--6) and ordinal decisions, each with a state, a rubric and an exact label set, in three
tiers: \emph{standard} (72 items), \emph{easy} (48) and \emph{hard} (111, eight families including long
policies, multi-hop, temporal, probability and trade-off). Its 146 judge-scored items are private and the
harness ranks only submissions covering 95\% of all items, so every JevBench number here is on the 231
public ids and unranked; no training corpus contains a public id. The 72 standard items are 36 states in
two paraphrases sharing a \texttt{group}, so intervals are cluster bootstraps over \texttt{group}
(20{,}000 resamples): $\pm6$--13 points on standard, $\pm9$ on hard. Comparisons between our models use
paired per-item bootstraps; scoring details and leaderboard context are in \Cref{app:leaderboard}.

\subsection{Hardware and latency}
\label{sec:setup:hardware}

Models train on single H100 GPUs (\Cref{app:hyperparams}). The \emph{research harness} times one in-process decision on a 256-token state, state encode included, for
$\numcands$ from 2 to 256, all sizes on one pod and PyTorch/CUDA build. The \emph{serving path} is the
released inference package, timed as warm p50 against an already-cached 256-token state at
$\numcands{=}2$.
